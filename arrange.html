Write a word, then move its glyphs into a pattern. The board only allows arrangements that can still be read. An experiment.

How it works

  • Drag a glyph to another square. While you hold it, the places it may go are lit. Drop it between two columns to give it a column of its own.
  • A full glyph is drawn as designed and has its square to itself. A flat glyph fills one quarter of a square, the quarter its vowel points away from, so up to four can share a square.
  • Drop a full glyph half a square to the side of its neighbour to nestle them: a triangle pointing up fits against one pointing down, as in ordinary writing.
  • A glyph cannot be turned or mirrored: the direction of the triangle is the vowel.
  • Read each word column by column, each column from the top, and each square left, top, bottom, right. The board refuses any move that would change that reading, put one glyph's marks inside another, or close the open side of u, e or o.
  • By keyboard: with the board in focus, [ and ] choose a glyph, the arrow keys move it, and F switches it between flat and full.

The squares, the flat form and these rules are this site's own, after the coloured rendering of the script's name on Wikipedia. They are not part of the script as published.
