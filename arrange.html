Write a word, then move its glyphs into a pattern. The board only allows arrangements that can still be read. An experiment.

How it works

  • The board is a lattice of the script's own triangle. A glyph that points up fits any triangle that points up, and a glyph that points down any that points down, at exactly its designed size. Side by side they share a side; one above the other they share a base or meet at a point.
  • Drag a glyph to another triangle. While you hold it, the places it may go are lit. The rest of its word comes along behind it, keeping its arrangement, so build a word from left to right. Hold Alt (Option) to move one glyph by itself.
  • e and o point sideways, which no triangle of the lattice does. They sit on a line instead, from one point of the lattice to the next, and so does the circle of a syllabic m or n. e and o can also be made large: two rows high, with every corner on the lattice.
  • A word is one figure: each glyph touches another at a point of the lattice. The board starts you off that way, with a leading vowel set a row above the rest of its word and a word of two glyphs stepping down.
  • A glyph cannot be turned or mirrored: the direction of the triangle is the vowel.
  • Read each word from left to right, and from the top where two glyphs start at the same place. The board refuses any move that would change that reading, make two glyphs overlap, put one glyph's marks where they could be read as another's, or close the open side of u, e or o. After each move it closes up any space left behind.
  • By keyboard: with the board in focus, [ and ] choose a glyph, the arrow keys move it with the glyphs after it (with Alt, by itself), and F switches e and o between small and large.

The lattice, these rules, and the forms of e, o and the syllabic circle here (a little longer or larger than designed, so that they meet the lattice) are this site's own. They are not part of the script as published.
